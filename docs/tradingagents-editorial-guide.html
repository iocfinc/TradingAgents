TradingAgents Editorial Guide

What this system actually does before it says “Buy” or “Sell.”

TradingAgents is not one model making a snap call. It is a staged argument. First, specialist analysts collect different kinds of evidence. Then opposing researchers debate whether the case is strong enough. Then a trader turns that argument into an execution plan. Then a risk committee debates the downside. Only after that does the portfolio manager issue the final verdict.

If you are not from finance, the easiest way to read the system is to separate three layers: evidence terms, recommendation terms, and action terms. “Oversold” is evidence. “Underweight” is a recommendation. “Sell” is an action.

Core flow
4 analysts -> 2 debates -> 1 final rating

Market, social, news, and fundamentals feed a bull/bear debate, then a second risk debate, before the final portfolio decision.

Final rating scale
Buy / Overweight / Hold / Underweight / Sell

These are the user-facing verdicts you should decode first. Everything else is supporting evidence or execution detail.

The flow, step by step

The system is deliberately layered so that one type of evidence does not dominate too early. Technical charts, news, balance sheet quality, and social mood all get their own voice before the debate starts.

01

Analyst intake

The Market Analyst reads price action and technical indicators. The Social Analyst reads sentiment. The News Analyst reads current events. The Fundamentals Analyst reads business and valuation signals.

02

Bull vs bear investment debate

Two researchers argue the upside case and the downside case. This is where the system tests whether the evidence deserves conviction or only caution.

03

Research Manager recommendation

The Research Manager collapses the argument into an investment recommendation on the five-step scale: Buy, Overweight, Hold, Underweight, or Sell.

04

Trader proposal

The Trader converts the recommendation into an execution plan: what to do now, at what kind of level, and with what kind of stop or sizing logic.

05

Risk debate

Aggressive, Conservative, and Neutral risk analysts debate whether the proposed trade overreaches, undershoots, or needs better guardrails.

06

Portfolio Manager final verdict

The Portfolio Manager issues the final user-facing decision. This is the verdict that gets logged and should be treated as the system’s official answer.

What “depth” really changes

In this checkout, depth is mainly a debate-length control. It does not add new analyst types or a different verdict scale.

Shallow One unit of investment debate and one unit of risk discussion. Fast pass, lower deliberation cost.
Medium More back-and-forth on both debate layers. Better when evidence is mixed and you want the opposing side to push harder.
Deep Longest argument loop. Best when you want a more contested final decision instead of the fastest one.

Keep this distinction straight

Evidence words Overbought, oversold, bullish crossover, support, resistance.
Recommendation words Buy, Overweight, Hold, Underweight, Sell.
Execution words Entry, stop-loss, trim, add, position size, time horizon.

How to read the final verdict

The recommendation scale is not pure chart jargon. It is a portfolio stance scale. Think of it as a slider from strong confidence to strong avoidance.

Buy

Strong positive view. The system thinks you should enter or add to the position now or soon, subject to the stated execution rules.

Overweight

Positive, but not maximum conviction. Own more than a normal baseline allocation, usually by adding gradually or on confirmed pullbacks.

Hold

Balanced or unresolved. Keep existing exposure, but do not treat the setup as strong enough to add aggressively.

Underweight

Cautious or mildly negative. Own less than a normal baseline allocation. This often means trimming rather than full exit.

Sell

Strong negative view. Exit, avoid entry, or, for some strategies, take the opposite side if the execution plan explicitly supports it.

Why this confuses non-finance readers

“Overweight” and “Underweight” sound like chart adjectives. They are not. They are portfolio allocation language: more than usual, or less than usual.

Important “Oversold” does not mean “Buy.” It means the price may have fallen a lot, fast. The system can still end in Hold or Underweight if the rest of the evidence stays weak.

The glossary for the reports you will actually read

These are the recurring report terms that most often block comprehension for non-finance readers. The plain-English reading is more important than the textbook definition.

ATR

Average True Range. A volatility gauge. Higher ATR means the price is swinging more each day, so stops usually need more room.

RSI

Relative Strength Index. A momentum measure often used to spot overbought or oversold conditions. It is a clue, not a verdict.

MACD

A momentum trend indicator built from moving averages. Traders use it to judge whether momentum is strengthening, weakening, or reversing.

MACD histogram

A quick read on momentum acceleration or fade. When it shrinks, the move is often losing force even if price has not fully turned.

SMA / EMA

Moving averages. SMA is smoother and slower. EMA reacts faster. They are often used as trend lines, support, or resistance zones.

Bollinger Bands

A volatility envelope around price. Touching the upper band can hint at heat or extension; touching the lower band can hint at stress or oversold conditions.

VWMA

Volume-Weighted Moving Average. Similar to a moving average, but it gives more influence to high-volume sessions, which can make the trend signal more grounded.

Support / resistance

Support is where buyers often appear. Resistance is where sellers often appear. A break through either level can change the whole trade setup.

Overbought / oversold

These describe stretched momentum, not automatic reversals. Overbought can stay overbought in a strong uptrend, and oversold can stay oversold in a strong downtrend.

P/E, P/B, FCF yield

Basic valuation shorthand. They ask whether the stock is expensive or cheap relative to earnings, book value, or cash generation.
